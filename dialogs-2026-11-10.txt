How they were made
------------------
Ten brand-new users were created, each with a rich public bio (what the other
person can know) and a private bio (hidden feelings, fears and goals). Every
pair met in its own room with a mutual handshake, and the agent worker drove
the whole conversation through the event queue: LLM-turn prompts, sentence
splitting into chat bubbles, echo filtering, and a farewell + goodbye-reply
pair at the end. Chats ran networking-only (the themed debate part is switched
off by BLEAK_NODE_AGENT_DEBATE=0), on the glm-5.3-flash model via an
OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

What works well
---------------
- Two-way balance: every turn of every chat follows the pattern "answer the
  partner's point + share one new detail about yourself + ask one open
  question back". Both sides discover each other's craft equally; nobody
  monologues.
- Facts come only from the bios (the HARD FACT RULE): Gleb keeps his paper
  route alive, Vera's mint died last winter, Kostya soldering slower every
  year, Lera's first failed truck on the shelf, Olya hunting sewing-machine
  parts, Fedya's dog co-pilot. When a topic falls outside their profile, the
  agents answer abstractly instead of inventing ("the season decides
  everything", "honestly I'm no timber expert").
- Real texture: small trade threads appear on their own (linseed oil for
  trams, cider for wooden toys, whole-vs-crumbled mint) and private-bio
  secrets leak softly the way they do in real life (the Belgian radio card,
  the first sold patch).
- Clean form: replies are two sentences at most, bubbles are short, nothing
  is cut mid-word, and all five dialogs close with a proper farewell /
  goodbye-reply pair, three of them answering the partner's last open
  question before leaving.

Known quirks (a next small prompt iteration could smooth them)
--------------------------------------------------------------
- A few filler answers stay abstract on model choice ("I found a specific
  type of wild herb") where a stronger model would produce a concrete crop.
- The turn seed occasionally pulls a "what are you working toward" question
  even when the goal was already covered (Vera/Anton), because it rotates by
  design; the answer-first rule only overrides it when a question is pending.
- Line 341 ("Honestly the free clinic beats paid work") shows the model
  forgetting terminal punctuation once in a while.
- One farewell (Fedya's) switches to the vague wrap-up before answering the
  partner's question, even though it was asked to do it in reverse order.
